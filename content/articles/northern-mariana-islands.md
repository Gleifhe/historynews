The Northern Mariana Islands — a chain of 14 islands in the western Pacific, roughly 1,500 miles south of Tokyo — occupy a unique place in American history. They were a Spanish colony, then a German colony, then a Japanese mandate territory, then a bloodsoaked battlefield, then a U.S.-administered trust territory, and finally — by choice — a commonwealth of the United States.

## The Battles

The Northern Marianas entered American consciousness through some of the most horrific fighting of World War II. The Battle of Saipan (June-July 1944) killed over 3,400 Americans and virtually the entire Japanese garrison of 30,000. An estimated 20,000 Japanese civilians on the island also died — many by suicide, leaping from the cliffs at the island's northern tip after Japanese propaganda convinced them that Americans would torture and kill them.

The capture of Tinian, the neighboring island, followed in July-August 1944. Tinian's significance would become clear a year later: on August 6, 1945, the Enola Gay took off from North Field on Tinian carrying the atomic bomb that destroyed Hiroshima.

## Trust Territory Years

After the war, the Northern Marianas became part of the [Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands](/articles/trust-territory-pacific/) administered by the United States under a UN mandate. The islands were governed by American administrators, and the local population — primarily Chamorro and Carolinian peoples — had limited self-governance.

Unlike other parts of the trust territory, the Northern Marianas had historical and cultural ties to nearby Guam (also Chamorro-majority) and expressed early interest in closer association with the United States rather than independence.

## The Choice

In 1975, the people of the Northern Mariana Islands voted in a UN-observed plebiscite to become a commonwealth of the United States. The vote was 78.8 percent in favor. The Covenant to Establish a Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands was signed in 1975 and took effect in 1978.

The commonwealth status gave the Northern Marianas a degree of self-government similar to Puerto Rico. Residents became U.S. citizens. They elected their own governor and legislature. But they could not vote in presidential elections and had only a non-voting delegate in Congress.

The decision to join the United States was pragmatic. The islands had few natural resources, limited economic prospects, and a population of roughly 16,000. Commonwealth status provided federal funding, military protection, and access to the American market. It also allowed the Northern Marianas to maintain some control over immigration and minimum wage — exemptions that would later prove controversial.

## The Garment Industry Controversy

In the 1980s and 1990s, the Northern Marianas became the center of a labor scandal that exposed the tensions in the islands' unique status. Taking advantage of the commonwealth's exemption from federal minimum wage and immigration laws, garment manufacturers imported tens of thousands of foreign workers — primarily from China, the Philippines, and Bangladesh — to sew clothing in island factories.

The clothes were labeled "Made in USA" because the Northern Marianas were U.S. territory, but the workers were paid far below mainland minimum wage and worked in conditions that labor advocates compared to sweatshops. Reports of forced labor, human trafficking, and worker abuse drew national attention.

Congress eventually extended federal minimum wage and immigration laws to the Northern Marianas through legislation in 2007 and 2008, ending the exemptions that had enabled the abuses.

## What This Means for You in 2026

The Northern Marianas' decision to join the United States was a calculated choice — driven by practical needs, not romantic idealism. They weighed their options, assessed the alternatives, and chose the path that offered the most security and opportunity, even with its limitations. When you face a major decision, be honest about your constraints and priorities. The best choice is not always the most inspiring one — sometimes it is simply the most realistic.
