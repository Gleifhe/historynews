## A City Cut in Half

To understand the night the Berlin Wall fell, you need to understand why it was built in the first place.

After World War II ended in 1945, Germany was divided into two countries. West Germany was democratic and free. East Germany was controlled by the Soviet Union and run as a communist state. The city of Berlin, which sat inside East Germany, was also split in two.

At first, people could move back and forth between East and West Berlin. But so many East Germans were escaping to the free West that the communist government decided to stop them. On August 13, 1961, they started building a wall.

The Berlin Wall was 96 miles long. It was made of concrete, topped with barbed wire, and guarded by soldiers with orders to shoot anyone who tried to cross. Over the next 28 years, at least 140 people died trying to get over, under, or through it.

## A Mistake on Live Television

The wall came down because of what might be the biggest accidental announcement in history.

By 1989, communist governments across Eastern Europe were falling apart. People in Poland, Hungary, and Czechoslovakia were demanding freedom. The pressure on East Germany's government was enormous.

On November 9, 1989, an East German official named Günter Schabowski held a press conference. He was supposed to announce that East Germans would soon be allowed to apply for travel visas. But he hadn't read the full memo carefully. When a reporter asked when the new travel rules would take effect, Schabowski shuffled through his papers and said: **"Immediately, without delay."**

That wasn't what the government had planned at all. But it was on live television. And people heard it.

## The Crowds Arrive

Within hours, thousands of East Berliners rushed to the wall's checkpoints. The border guards had received no instructions about what to do. They called their commanders. Nobody answered. The crowds kept growing.

Finally, at the Bornholmer Straße crossing, a guard named Harald Jäger made a decision. Overwhelmed by the sheer number of people — and worried about violence — he ordered the gates opened.

The crowd surged through. People were crying, hugging strangers, and shouting with joy. Many had never been to West Berlin in their entire lives. East Berliners climbed on top of the wall and started chipping away at the concrete with hammers, pickaxes, and their bare hands. People brought champagne. West Berliners pulled East Berliners up onto the wall and embraced them.

## The World's Home Videos

What makes the Europeana collection special is that it gathers the personal stories — the photos, diaries, and amateur videos from ordinary people who were there that night.

One diary entry from a woman in East Berlin reads: "I walked through the gate and looked at the lights. I had never seen so many lights. I stood on the street and cried because I didn't know where to go. I was free and I had no idea what to do with it."

Home videos show families reunited for the first time in decades. One clip shows an elderly woman meeting her sister on the Western side. They hadn't seen each other in 27 years.

## What Happened Next

The wall didn't disappear overnight. Over the following weeks and months, sections were torn down by bulldozers and cranes. People took pieces as souvenirs — chunks of painted concrete that are now displayed in museums around the world.

On October 3, 1990 — less than a year after the wall fell — East and West Germany officially reunited as one country. The Cold War was ending. The Soviet Union itself would collapse just two years later, in 1991.

## Why It Still Matters

The fall of the Berlin Wall is one of the most important moments of the 20th century. It showed that ordinary people, armed with nothing but courage and determination, could bring down a system that had tanks, soldiers, and secret police.

The Europeana collection reminds us that this wasn't just a political event — it was millions of personal stories. Families torn apart. People who risked their lives for freedom. And one incredible night when the impossible happened.
