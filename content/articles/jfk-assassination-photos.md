## 12:30 PM, Dallas, Texas

November 22, 1963. President John F. Kennedy's motorcade was rolling through downtown Dallas, Texas, in an open-top limousine. The sun was shining. Crowds lined the streets. The president and First Lady Jacqueline Kennedy were waving and smiling.

At 12:30 p.m., as the motorcade passed through Dealey Plaza, shots rang out. President Kennedy was hit. The limousine raced to Parkland Memorial Hospital. At 1:00 p.m., President Kennedy was pronounced dead.

He was 46 years old.

## The Most Documented Murder in History

What makes the Kennedy assassination unique is how thoroughly it was documented. Dealey Plaza was crowded with people, and many of them had cameras.

Professional photographers riding in the press cars behind Kennedy captured the motorcade moments before and after the shots. Amateur photographers and home movie enthusiasts on the sidewalks captured the scene from multiple angles.

The result is the most photographed and filmed murder in history — and, ironically, one of the most debated.

## The Zapruder Film

The most famous piece of evidence is a 26-second home movie shot by Abraham Zapruder, a Dallas clothing manufacturer. Zapruder was standing on a concrete pedestal with his 8mm Bell & Howell camera, filming the motorcade as a souvenir.

Instead, he captured the assassination — frame by frame.

The Zapruder film shows the exact moments of the shooting. Frame 313 — the frame that shows the fatal head shot — became one of the most analyzed images in history. Experts have studied every frame, every shadow, every detail, looking for clues.

Life magazine purchased the film for $150,000 (about $1.4 million today) and published individual frames, but the complete film wasn't shown to the public on television until 1975.

## The Press Photographers

Several professional photographers were in the motorcade:

- **James "Ike" Altgens**, an Associated Press photographer standing on the south side of Elm Street, captured a photo just 2 seconds after the first shot. His photo shows Kennedy clutching his throat and Secret Service agent Clint Hill beginning to sprint toward the limousine.

- **Tom Dillard** of the Dallas Morning News photographed the Texas School Book Depository building moments after the shots, showing two open windows — one of which was allegedly the sniper's position.

- **Robert Croft**, a tourist, captured the motorcade just moments before the first shot.

These photos — many available through Getty Images, AP, and other archives — provide crucial visual evidence about timing, positions, and angles.

## Other Films and Photos

At least 32 photographers are known to have been taking pictures or shooting film in Dealey Plaza that day:

- **Orville Nix** captured the assassination from the opposite side of the street from Zapruder, providing a different angle
- **Marie Muchmore** filmed from the south side, showing the motorcade from behind
- **Mary Moorman** took a famous Polaroid photograph at almost the exact instant of the fatal shot

Each new piece of visual evidence has been studied, enhanced, debated, and argued over for decades.

## Visual Evidence and Conspiracy Theories

The photographic evidence has both clarified and complicated the assassination investigation.

The **Warren Commission** (1964) concluded that Lee Harvey Oswald acted alone, firing three shots from the sixth floor of the Texas School Book Depository.

But the visual evidence has fueled conspiracy theories. Some investigators have claimed to see:
- Additional shooters in photographs
- Suspicious figures on the "grassy knoll"
- Evidence of shots from multiple directions

The **House Select Committee on Assassinations** (1979) reviewed the evidence and concluded that Kennedy "was probably assassinated as a result of a conspiracy," though later analysis questioned their acoustic evidence.

## The Photos That Changed Journalism

The JFK assassination changed how news was covered:

- **Television became the primary news source**: For four straight days, all three networks broadcast commercial-free coverage. Over 93% of American households watched. It was the moment TV replaced newspapers as America's main source of news.
- **The role of amateur photographers grew**: Before Dallas, news photography was the domain of professionals. After Dallas, the value of eyewitness footage became clear.
- **Visual evidence became central to investigations**: The Zapruder film showed that moving images could be analyzed frame by frame as forensic evidence.

## A Story Without an Ending

More than 60 years later, polls consistently show that a majority of Americans don't believe the official explanation of the assassination. The photographic and film evidence is endlessly reanalyzed.

The National Archives has millions of pages of assassination-related documents. The JFK Records Act of 1992 ordered the release of all records by 2017, though some remain classified.

The photographs from Dealey Plaza — preserved in the Library of Congress, Getty Images, the National Archives, and the Sixth Floor Museum — capture the last moments of a presidency and the beginning of an endless debate.
